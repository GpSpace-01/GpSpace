JWST and the Early Universe

Webb observes infrared light, which is especially useful for studying distant galaxies and cool astronomical objects. Because cosmic expansion stretches travelling light toward longer wavelengths, infrared observations can reveal information about very distant objects. The full guide explains how astronomers interpret those observations rather than treating a telescope image as a simple photograph.

Black Holes Explained

A black hole is defined by the presence of an event horizon, not by a visible surface. Astronomers study black holes through their gravitational influence, hot material around them, stellar motion and gravitational-wave signals. The GpSpace guide explains what is observed, what is inferred and what remains uncertain.

Space Weather

Solar flares and coronal mass ejections are different phenomena, although both can be associated with active regions on the Sun. When solar disturbances interact with Earth's magnetosphere, they can produce auroras and can also create conditions relevant to satellites, radio communication and electrical infrastructure.

Roman Space Telescope

Roman is designed as a wide-field infrared observatory. Its surveys will cover large areas of sky and support research into dark energy, galaxy evolution and exoplanets. The GpSpace guide explains the mission's scientific goals and why a wide field of view complements other space telescopes.

Earth's Magnetic Field

Earth's magnetic field is generated largely by moving electrically conducting material in the outer core. It deflects much of the solar wind and helps shape the magnetosphere. The field is not fixed: its strength and direction vary over time, and the magnetic poles move.

How Old Is the Universe?

Cosmologists estimate the age of the universe using observations such as cosmic expansion and the cosmic microwave background together with models of cosmic evolution. The estimate is not based on simply finding the oldest star; it comes from fitting observations to a physical model.
