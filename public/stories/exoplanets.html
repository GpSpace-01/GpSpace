How Do We Find Planets Around Other Stars?

GpSpace • Astronomy guide

Exoplanets are planets orbiting stars beyond our Solar System. Most are too faint and too close to their bright host stars to photograph directly, so astronomers often detect them through their effects on starlight or stellar motion.

The transit method

When a planet crosses in front of its star from our viewpoint, the star's brightness dips slightly. Repeating dips can reveal the planet's orbital period and provide information about its size relative to the star.

The radial-velocity method

A planet's gravity makes its star move around a common center of mass. This motion can produce a small, repeating shift in the star's spectrum. The size and pattern of the signal can reveal properties of the planet's orbit and minimum mass.

Can we find life?

Finding an exoplanet is only the beginning. Studying an atmosphere for possible chemical signatures requires careful observations and multiple lines of evidence. A single molecule would not automatically prove life.
