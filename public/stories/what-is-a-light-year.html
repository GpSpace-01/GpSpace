What Is a Light-Year?

GpSpace • Space & science explainer

A light-year measures distance, not time. Because light travels at about 300,000 kilometres per second in vacuum, one light-year is an enormous distance.

What should you know?

Astronomers use light-years because kilometres become unwieldy when describing distances between stars and galaxies. A star several light-years away is seen as it was several years in the past.

This is why looking farther into space also means looking further back in cosmic history.
