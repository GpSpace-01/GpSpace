Space Weather: When the Sun Gets Active

GpSpace • Sun & technology guide

Space weather describes changing conditions in near-Earth space driven mainly by solar activity. The Sun continuously releases the solar wind, and occasional eruptions can send much stronger disturbances toward Earth.

Solar flares

Solar flares are sudden releases of electromagnetic energy. Strong flares can increase high-energy radiation and can affect radio communication on the sunlit side of Earth.

Coronal mass ejections

A coronal mass ejection, or CME, is a huge cloud of magnetized plasma launched from the Sun. If a CME reaches Earth and interacts strongly with the magnetosphere, it can trigger geomagnetic storms and bright auroras.

Why should we care?

Modern society depends on satellites, navigation, radio communication and large electrical networks. Space-weather forecasting and monitoring help reduce operational risks during strong solar events.
