How the James Webb Space Telescope Sees the Early Universe

GpSpace • Astronomy guide

The James Webb Space Telescope is a large infrared observatory designed to study faint and distant objects. Because light takes time to travel, observing very distant galaxies lets astronomers see them as they appeared when the universe was younger.

Why infrared?

As the universe expands, light from distant galaxies is stretched toward longer wavelengths. This redshift can move light into the infrared, where Webb is particularly sensitive. Infrared observations also help astronomers see through some dust that blocks shorter-wavelength light.

What can Webb reveal?

Webb can study the formation of stars and planets, distant galaxies, the atmospheres of some exoplanets and many other targets. Its observations complement those from other observatories rather than replacing them.

Does Webb literally see the beginning of the universe?

No telescope can see the instant of the Big Bang. Webb looks back toward increasingly early cosmic times by observing light that has travelled for billions of years. Each distant observation is therefore also a look into the past.
