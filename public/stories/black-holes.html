BLACK HOLES

Black Holes Explained: What Is Really Happening?

GpSpace • Astronomy guide

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that, beyond its event horizon, nothing can escape to the outside—not even light. It is not an empty hole in space. A black hole is described by the mass and other properties of the compact object that created it.

What is the event horizon?

The event horizon is a boundary, not a solid surface. For a distant observer, it marks the point beyond which signals cannot return. The exact size of this boundary depends on the black hole's mass.

How do we see something that is black?

Astronomers usually observe the environment around a black hole. Hot gas in an accretion disk can shine strongly, while jets can extend far beyond the central object. Scientists can also infer black holes from the motion of nearby stars and gas or from gravitational waves produced when compact objects merge.

Are black holes cosmic vacuum cleaners?

No. At the same distance, a black hole's gravity behaves like the gravity of any other object with the same mass. Objects only fall in if their paths bring them sufficiently close.
